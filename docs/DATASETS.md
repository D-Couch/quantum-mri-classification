# Dataset registry

## Cheng et al. brain tumor dataset (Figshare)

- **Source:** [Figshare v5](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.1512427.v5)
- **Download files:** [Part 1](https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/3381290), [part 2](https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/3381296), [part 3](https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/3381293), [part 4](https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/3381302), [published fold IDs](https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/7005344).
- **License:** CC BY 4.0, as shown on the [Figshare record](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/brain_tumor_data-set/1512427/5?report=reader).
- **Provenance:** Cheng et al. report 3,064 T1-weighted contrast-enhanced 2D slices from 233 patients acquired at two hospitals from 2005–2010. The dataset contains 708 meningioma, 1,426 glioma, and 930 pituitary tumor samples; patient information was anonymized. The paper describes partitioning patients into five folds to keep each patient's slices together.
- **Original fields:** MATLAB `.mat` records contain image, label, patient ID, tumor boundary and mask. Label mapping is `1=meningioma`, `2=glioma`, `3=pituitary`.
- **Limitation:** This dataset has no no-tumor class. It must be reported as a three-class problem. The study's original results used handcrafted ROI/feature methods and its fold protocol; they are not directly comparable to this platform's image-level models unless the same protocol is reproduced.
- **Import:** Extract the v5 `.mat` files and call `mri_classifier.figshare.import_figshare_directory(source_dir, output_dir, cvind_path)`. The importer creates grayscale images, `manifest.csv` with patient IDs and published fold IDs, and `dataset.json`. It does not download or commit the source data.
- **Integrity and protocol:** The importer checks patient IDs against the official `cvind.mat` five-fold assignment. The trainer can hold one fold for test and the next for validation, with remaining folds for training. For other datasets it offers deterministic patient-group holdout. Manifest paths must stay under the image root and byte-identical images are rejected.
- **Import verification:** All four archive MD5s matched official Figshare API metadata. Import yielded 3,064 readable images, 233 patient IDs, expected class counts, and zero patient/fold conflicts.
- **Near-duplicate screening:** The read-only command `python -m mri_classifier.near_duplicates --manifest data/figshare-v5/manifest.csv --image-root data/figshare-v5` compares 64-bit difference hashes (distance ≤3) and flags cross-patient/cross-fold candidates with global SSIM ≥0.90 on 128×128 grayscale thumbnails for manual review. On v5 it found 80 cross-patient/cross-fold hash candidates and two SSIM review candidates. Both were visually reviewed and had distinct lesion structures; no near-duplicate was confirmed. This heuristic is not proof that all near-duplicates are absent. Official patient-disjoint folds remain the primary leakage safeguard. The ignored local review output is `artifacts/near-duplicate-review.json` and contains filenames only, not patient identifiers.
- **Initial measured baseline (preliminary, not a final benchmark):** ResNet18 trained from scratch, seed 42, official fold 5 held out for test, fold 1 for validation, and the remaining folds for training. Checkpoint selection used validation macro-F1; early stopping ended after 8 epochs, with best epoch 3. On the 643-image test fold: accuracy 0.829, macro-F1 0.822, balanced accuracy 0.831, macro ROC-AUC 0.927, and macro average precision 0.850. Runtime was 339 seconds on CPU. This is one seed and one fold with no independent replication, so it is preliminary and not directly comparable with Cheng et al.'s reported results.
- **Initial transfer result (preliminary, separate fold):** ResNet18 initialized with torchvision's default ImageNet weights (`ResNet18_Weights.DEFAULT`), seed 42, official fold 4 held out for test, fold 5 for validation, and folds 1–3 for training. Validation macro-F1 selected the checkpoint; early stopping ended after 8 epochs, with best epoch 3. On the 628-image test fold: accuracy 0.946, macro-F1 0.929, balanced accuracy 0.921, macro ROC-AUC 0.994, and macro average precision 0.983. Runtime was 71.6 seconds on CPU. The held-out fold differs from the scratch run, so these scores are not a direct model comparison; both are single-seed preliminary results without uncertainty estimates.

  **Validation-based family comparison:** Across all five validation folds and three seeds per fold (15 runs per family), mean validation macro-F1 ± sample SD was 0.835 ± 0.043 for scratch ResNet18 (range 0.761–0.896), 0.931 ± 0.027 for ImageNet transfer ResNet18 (0.879–0.966), and 0.492 ± 0.036 for the hybrid (0.456–0.578). ImageNet transfer had the highest mean validation macro-F1 on each of the five fold configurations, so it is the selected leading family for this fixed pilot. This is not proof of general superiority; no family or hyperparameter was selected using test scores.

  Across all 15 runs, validation macro-F1 was 0.835 ± 0.043 for ResNet18 and 0.841 ± 0.049 for EfficientNet-B0. EfficientNet's fold-mean advantage was small (+0.006) and it had larger seed variation; each architecture had the higher validation mean on some folds. The five-fold patient bootstrap below found a paired EfficientNet-minus-ResNet out-of-fold macro-F1 difference of −0.006 (95% interval −0.024 to 0.012), a descriptive test result that was not used to select the architecture. The transfer ResNet18 remained the leading family by validation macro-F1 (0.931 ± 0.027 across all folds and seeds). More architecture and transfer-learning comparisons remain appropriate before a broad claim.

  **Transfer architecture pilot (validation only):** On test fold 1 / validation fold 2, EfficientNet-B0 ImageNet transfer with seeds 42–44 reached validation macro-F1 0.918 ± 0.006, compared with 0.921 ± 0.004 for matched ResNet18 ImageNet transfer runs. This three-seed, single-fold result did not improve on ResNet18, so EfficientNet transfer was not extended across the other folds. The held-out test scores were not used for this decision.

  **Tumor-mask supervision pilot (validation only):** The importer now retains the existing per-image tumor masks. The leading ImageNet-transfer ResNet18 was kept as the classifier: an FPN-style mask head receives auxiliary binary-cross-entropy plus Dice loss (weight 0.2), and its predicted mask weights an ROI-pooled feature alongside global features. Ground-truth masks are used only for training supervision; validation, test, and single-image inference use the predicted mask. On all five official patient folds and seeds 42–44 (15 matched runs), validation macro-F1 was **0.943 ± 0.021**, versus **0.931 ± 0.027** for the existing transfer ResNet18 pilot. Fold means were 0.951 vs 0.921 (fold 1), 0.931 vs 0.930 (fold 2), 0.966 vs 0.961 (fold 3), 0.915 vs 0.889 (fold 4), and 0.955 vs 0.954 (fold 5). The mask-supervised run had the higher fold mean on all five folds; the equal-fold aggregate gain was +0.013 macro-F1. Recipe: 10 epochs maximum, batch size 32, learning rate 0.0001, seeds 42–44, no augmentation, and checkpoint selection by validation macro-F1. Held-out test metrics were not inspected or used for selection. This is a fixed-protocol internal pilot, not a clinical result or an external/state-of-the-art comparison; the fold 2 and fold 3 baseline records came from earlier code revisions, though the ResNet18 training/model recipe was unchanged. The new mask-bearing dataset copy and all checkpoints/reports remain ignored under `artifacts/`; the original dataset was left untouched. The dashboard now defaults to transfer mode and mask supervision for compatible ResNet18 runs while keeping the option available to turn off.

  **Follow-up validation screens (test scores not accessed):** To improve on the mask-supervised ResNet18, three one-fold screens were run on official test fold 1 / validation fold 2 with the same dataset, 10-epoch limit, batch size 32, training seed set (42–44), and CUDA/FP32 setting. Lowering the pretrained encoder learning rate from 0.0001 to 0.00001 while leaving the heads at 0.0001 produced validation macro-F1 0.925, 0.923, and 0.924 (mean **0.924**), below the established 224-pixel model's same-fold three-seed mean of **0.951**. Reducing the mask loss multiplier from 0.2 to 0.05 produced 0.924, 0.947, and 0.945 (mean **0.939**). Increasing input resolution from 224 to 320 produced 0.946, 0.945, and 0.951 (mean **0.947**). None advanced to a full five-fold evaluation or changed the recommended model. These are screening results from one validation fold and do not establish that the rejected settings are universally worse. The runs and checkpoint files are in ignored local `artifacts/`; the runner generated final test reports as part of its workflow, but those test metrics were not opened, analyzed, or used to decide.

  **Synchronized mask augmentation screen (validation only):** Added training-only horizontal flip and ±10° rotation with the same sampled geometry applied to each image and its tumor mask (bilinear image interpolation, nearest-neighbor mask interpolation). On test fold 1 / validation fold 2 with the mask-supervised ImageNet ResNet18, 224-pixel input, batch size 32, 10-epoch limit, and seeds 42–44, validation macro-F1 was **0.930, 0.936, 0.945** (mean **0.937**). The unaugmented matched-fold mean was **0.951**. This candidate was not advanced. The comparison uses the same patient split and recipe, but a three-seed single-fold screen is not evidence that augmentation is harmful on all folds. Configurations and checkpoints are retained under ignored `artifacts/mask-aux-augmentation-screen/`; final test metrics were not inspected.

  **Patient probability pooling screen (validation only):** For the same mask-supervised ResNet18 checkpoints with test fold 1 / validation fold 2, averaged each patient's slice probability vectors and scored one prediction per patient. Across the 46 held-out validation patients, macro-F1 was **0.957, 0.934, 0.941** for seeds 42–44 (mean **0.944**). This provides an initial patient-output baseline, but one fold and three seeds are insufficient to establish a patient-level improvement or meet the 0.99 target; the image-level model-selection protocol is unchanged. This simple pooling screen does not justify implementing attention MIL yet. Test-set predictions were not evaluated for this analysis.

  **RadImageNet transfer screen (validation only):** A separate local screen compared mask-supervised ResNet50 initialized from ImageNet versus RadImageNet on test fold 1 / validation fold 2, with seeds 42–44 and the same data partition. ImageNet validation macro-F1 was **0.957, 0.937, 0.929** (mean **0.941**); RadImageNet was **0.861, 0.887, 0.871** (mean **0.873**). RadImageNet was not extended. The screen does not support the assumption that medical-image pretraining automatically improves this task; the exact local records and source checkpoint details are in ignored `artifacts/radimagenet-resnet50-screen/`. Test results were not inspected or used. The standard ResNet18 remains the leading validated model.

  **External dataset candidate assessment (no data imported):** BRISC 2025 provides 6,000 four-class 2D images and expert-reviewed masks for a subset. Its paper says the dataset was assembled from multiple public collections, including the Cheng/Figshare brain-tumor data used above, and that complete subject-level independence cannot be guaranteed. Its released manifest has filenames, class, plane, split, and image metadata, but no patient identifiers. Thus it cannot currently provide additional *verified patient-disjoint* training/evaluation cases for this project; the unknown source membership also prevents reliable exclusion of the Figshare-overlapping subset. Do not combine its public image-level split with the existing folds or use it to claim patient-held-out performance. Reassess if the authors provide patient/source identifiers or a demonstrably independent cohort. The release and limitations are described in the [BRISC dataset paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-06753-y); its [data record](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/briscdataset/brisc2025) is the cited distribution source.

  **Vision Transformer pilot (validation only):** Added torchvision ViT-B/16 with ImageNet-1K pretrained weights as an optional classical architecture. On test fold 1 / validation fold 2, 224-pixel inputs, 10 epochs maximum, learning rate 0.0001, and seeds 42–44, fully fine-tuned ViT-B/16 reached validation macro-F1 **0.933 ± 0.003**. This is below the mask-supervised ResNet18's 0.951 mean on the same fold. The ViT was not advanced to the remaining folds and is not the recommended model. This small, one-fold screen does not rule out other transformer encoders, frozen-feature or partial fine-tuning, or medically pretrained weights. The torchvision model definition and weights are documented in the [official model entry](https://docs.pytorch.org/vision/stable/models/generated/torchvision.models.vit_b_16.html); checkpoints and reports stay under ignored `artifacts/`. As with the other screens, test outputs generated by the runner were not inspected or used.

  **Swin-T and ConvNeXt-Tiny pilots (validation only):** Fully fine-tuned torchvision ImageNet-pretrained Swin-T reached macro-F1 0.949, 0.941, and 0.951 across seeds 42–44 (mean **0.947**); ConvNeXt-Tiny reached 0.944, 0.946, and 0.957 (mean **0.949**). Both use the same test-fold-1 / validation-fold-2 split and 10-epoch recipe as the ViT screen. Neither exceeded the mask-supervised ResNet18's three-seed fold mean of 0.951. Mean probabilities from the three ResNet18 seeds alone scored 0.962 validation macro-F1; a 75% ResNet18 / 25% ConvNeXt probability blend scored 0.964, while equal weighting scored 0.963. This small single-fold ensemble gain is not evidence for a five-fold gain or for 0.99 performance; neither architecture nor blend is promoted. Test outputs were not inspected or used.

  **Validation error and source-record audit (validation only):** On the 679 images in fold 2, the three mask-supervised ResNet18 checkpoints made 26, 34, and 33 errors, with macro-F1 0.956, 0.947, and 0.949. Errors were concentrated in 11–14 held-out patients per seed, and several of the same patients had errors for all three seeds. Changing random initialization or switching to the screened ViT did not solve them. All 679 fold-2 masks were present, dimension-matched, and nonempty. To check the importer, 35 recurrently misclassified samples were extracted from four official Figshare v5 archive parts; all four archive MD5 checksums matched Figshare's API. For all 35, source labels, patient IDs, normalized PNG pixels, and binarized mask pixels exactly matched the manifest and imported files. This rules out an importer/record mismatch in these cases, but it does not establish that the source diagnoses themselves are clinically correct. Per-image predictions and patient identifiers remain local; held-out test cases were not examined. Do not drop difficult but valid cases to raise the score. Further progress likely needs a stronger or differently pretrained model, additional curated patient data, or expert review of ambiguous cases, evaluated on the remaining patient folds and external patients.

  **Training-only augmentation pilot (validation only):** ImageNet-transfer ResNet18, test fold 1 / validation fold 2, 10 epochs, batch size 32, learning rate 0.0001, seeds 42–44. Training images received random horizontal flips (p=0.5) and rotations up to 10 degrees; validation/test transforms were unchanged. Matched unaugmented validation macro-F1 was 0.921 ± 0.004; augmentation reached 0.924 ± 0.006. Paired seed differences were +0.0098, +0.0032, and −0.0024. This small, mixed one-fold result is inconclusive and was not used to inspect or select on final test scores. It does not justify extending the augmentation setting as an improvement claim. All runs are labeled `experiment_kind=smoke`; config, validation metrics, and checkpoints are in the ignored SQLite/artifact store.

  Across the five folds, the unweighted mean of each fold's three-seed test macro-F1 mean was 0.834 for scratch ResNet18, 0.920 for ImageNet transfer ResNet18, and 0.490 for the hybrid. The sample SD across the five fold means was 0.023, 0.019, and 0.010, respectively. These describe fold spread, not confidence intervals; folds and patient groups are not independent replicates. The test summaries are descriptive and were not used for model selection. This fixed three-seed protocol is a research pilot, not a clinical result or directly comparable Cheng et al. benchmark.

  **Patient-cluster uncertainty:** `python -m mri_classifier.patient_bootstrap` re-evaluates the saved fixed-cohort checkpoints, pools out-of-fold predictions across the five fold-specific models for each seed, then averages macro-F1 across seeds. A class-stratified bootstrap resamples the 233 patient groups (89 glioma, 82 meningioma, 62 pituitary) 2,000 times, keeping all slices from each sampled patient together. The pooled macro-F1 and 95% percentile interval were 0.835 (0.805–0.863) for scratch ResNet18, 0.920 (0.892–0.944) for ImageNet transfer, 0.829 (0.801–0.856) for scratch EfficientNet-B0, and 0.492 (0.472–0.512) for the hybrid. Paired bootstrap differences versus transfer were −0.085 (−0.104 to −0.068) for scratch ResNet18, −0.090 (−0.109 to −0.074) for EfficientNet-B0, and −0.428 (−0.455 to −0.400) for hybrid. The paired EfficientNet-minus-ResNet18 scratch difference was −0.006 (−0.024 to 0.012). The analysis used held-out predictions for descriptive uncertainty only; it did not tune models. Intervals condition on the saved checkpoints and three seeds, and do not measure training-seed, source-population, or clinical uncertainty. The reproducible summary is written to ignored `artifacts/patient-cluster-bootstrap.json`; patient identifiers and per-image predictions are not written to that summary.

The FP16 and FP32 ranges overlap, so this profile shows no clear AMP speed benefit. Repeat with warm-up and compare multi-epoch validation behavior before choosing defaults. Effective device, workers, pinned-memory state, and AMP activation are recorded in each report; all 12 successful run records are in the local SQLite index. The dashboard defaults to automatic device selection, zero workers, and AMP off.

  **Multi-epoch GPU timing check (smoke only):** ResNet18 scratch, batch size 32, four epochs, official test fold 1 / validation fold 2, seeds 42–44 (three runs per setting). Treating epoch 1 as warm-up, the nine remaining epoch times per setting had medians (ranges) of 9.12 s (8.64–9.78) with zero workers / FP32, 3.77 s (3.70–4.10) with two workers / FP32, and 8.81 s (8.05–9.58) with zero workers / FP16 autocast. The two-worker improvement repeated across all three seeds; keep it configurable because performance may vary by machine and dataset. AMP timing overlaps FP32 and shows no clear speed benefit. Validation curves varied across seeds and settings, so the short runs do not establish model-quality or precision stability. Test metrics were not inspected for this profile. All nine run records are in SQLite and labeled `experiment_kind=smoke`.

## Pretrained model-weight provenance

- Torchvision 0.29.1 resolves `EfficientNet_B0_Weights.DEFAULT` to `IMAGENET1K_V1`. Torchvision identifies these as ImageNet-1K weights ported from the original paper, and documents their checkpoint URL and inference transforms in its [EfficientNet-B0 model entry](https://docs.pytorch.org/vision/main/models/generated/torchvision.models.efficientnet_b0). The weight implementation points to [PyTorch Image Models](https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models) as its source.
- Torchvision warns that pretrained weights can carry terms derived from their training dataset and says users must determine permitted use; see its [pretrained model license note](https://github.com/pytorch/vision/blob/main/README.md#pre-trained-model-license). ImageNet-derived weights are used here for research evaluation only; this record does not establish commercial-use permission.
- The transfer pilot uses `torchvision.models.EfficientNet_B0_Weights.IMAGENET1K_V1`, from `https://download.pytorch.org/models/efficientnet_b0_rwightman-7f5810bc.pth`, with torchvision 0.29.1+cu130. New transfer reports record the exact weight enum, checkpoint URL, and installed torchvision version. Earlier reports used the `DEFAULT` alias and record ImageNet provenance but predate this more detailed metadata.
